# User agent review: Racking FR, round 1 (2026-10-29)

**Role:** User agent, the impatient professional (CLAUDE.md §4). I'm an operations manager at a mid-size manufacturer in Beauce. I tapped a Facebook video ad between meetings: « Votre entrepôt est plein… ou simplement mal optimisé? »
**Build tested:** existing `_site/` (not rebuilt), `/racking/` and `/racking/merci/`.
**Runs:**
1. **Mobile:** 390×844, DSF 2, touch, Android UA, fr-CA. Real first visit with no consent pre-set and UTMs in the URL. I skimmed in bursts of 400–700px with a 0.9s pause and took a viewport screenshot at each stop. Then I tapped the hero CTA, filled the form with a 9-digit phone, fixed it, submitted, and looked at the thank-you page. Script: `qa/shots/user-r1/run-mobile.mjs`, log: `run-mobile.log`.
2. **Desktop:** 1440×900, mouse wheel, same skim. I clicked the gallery arrows to the end, opened the FAQ that matters to me, took the header CTA, entered a bad email, fixed it and submitted. Script: `run-desktop.mjs`, log: `run-desktop.log`.
3. Harness section shots at 390 and 1440 (`section-*-390/1440.png`, `full-*.png`) and section measurements (`measure.mjs`).

All screenshots are in `qa/shots/user-r1/`. There were no console errors on either run and no horizontal overflow.

---

## Verdict in one paragraph

I'd have converted, but on mobile it was closer than it should be. The headline matches the ad word for word, so I knew in 3 seconds I was in the right place. The sticky « Consultation gratuite » button and the phone icon were on screen at every single stop, and the form is short, behaves well and forgives mistakes. What nearly lost me on my phone was the first screen: a wall of text, a privacy sheet over the button, and not one rack in sight. After that came about 3,200px of icon cards telling me things I already know. On desktop it's a solid, professional page. What's missing everywhere is **proof**. I see logos and « 50 ans », but no named project, no result and no customer saying it worked.

---

## 1. The 5-second impression (mobile, `m01-first-viewport-5s.png`)

- **Understood:** Pedlex does pallet racking, it's about a full or badly organised warehouse, and they offer a free consultation. The H1 is the ad's line, which is good.
- **Saw:** the logo, a big headline, a sub-headline, **two more paragraphs**, then the main button half-covered by the « Votre vie privée » sheet (226px tall, starting at y=602; the CTA runs from 557 to 613).
- **Didn't see:** a single photo. The rack photo starts at y=804, under the fold and under the banner. I came from a *video* of warehouses and landed on what looks like a text document.
- **The banner:** I tapped « Refuser » without reading, as everyone does. Credit where due: Refuser and Accepter get equal weight and it isn't a modal. Once it was gone, the screen looked calmer (`m02-after-decline.png`), but still no photo.
- **Desktop** (`d01-first-viewport.png`) is the opposite: the headline on the left, a big real rack photo on the right, the CTA and phone number visible. Within 5 seconds I knew who they were and what they do. The banner sits low and overlaps nothing important.

## 2. Where I nearly bounced

1. **The first mobile screen** (`m01`, `m02`): no image, three text blocks, and the cookie sheet over the button. On a train I'd scroll once, and if the next screen didn't hook me I'd be gone.
2. **The problem cards on mobile** (`m05` → `m07`): « Plus de capacité », « Un meilleur accès à l'inventaire », « Des opérations plus fluides », « Un entreposage adapté… ». They're four full-width cards, 1,524px for the whole section. I know what racking does. Three flicks of my thumb and nothing new.
3. **« Pourquoi Pedlex » on mobile** (`m11` → `m13`): six more full-width cards (1,714px). « 50 ans d'expertise », « clé en main » and « sur mesure » are the third time I've read each one (hero trust line → stats → here). By this point I was scrolling without reading. The form starts **7,657px down a 10,453px page**, about 9 screens.
4. **The FAQ answer I actually cared about** (`d30-faq-open.png`): « Est-ce que Pedlex peut optimiser un système existant? » gets « Oui. Pedlex peut analyser votre configuration actuelle… ». That's a yes with no substance. Can you reuse my uprights? Do you inspect damaged frames? (That's copy, so it's a client question, not a build fix.)

What pulled me back: the stats band (`m04`; « 40 % » is the one number that hooks me), the gallery's real photos (`m08`), and the installer on the scissor lift (`m09`). That photo says « these guys actually install » better than any paragraph.

## 3. Did I trust Pedlex?

**Mostly yes, as a supplier. Not yet as *my* supplier.**

Built trust:
- Real photos of real installs, nothing that smells of stock. The installer in a harness on the lift is the best image on the page.
- Client logos I recognise (STM, Globocam, Nordic, Omnifab), the Anjou address, the RBQ licence, a 514 number and a toll-free number. It's a real company with a real shop.
- The phone number is always one tap away (header icon, hero, form section, FAQ, footer).
- « Réponse d'un expert en 1 jour ouvrable » and « Sans frais ni engagement » right beside the form. That's exactly what I want to know before I give my number.

Hurt trust:
- **No proof that it worked for someone like me.** There's no testimonial and no « Client X, Drummondville, +620 positions ». The only result is « jusqu'à 40 % … dans certains projets », and the hedge makes me read it as « once ».
- **Gallery captions are product types, not projects** (« Palettiers sélectifs », « Centre de distribution »). Whose warehouse? Where? What changed?
- **« Accréditations en santé et sécurité » with the CNESST logo** (`m13`, `d09`). CNESST is the regulator that inspects *me*; it doesn't accredit anyone. To someone who deals with CNESST inspectors, that line reads like borrowed authority. SafeContractor and ComplyWorks are fine.
- **The last two gallery photos** (`d22-gallery-end.png`) show bleach jugs on red-beam shelving and a green pegboard wall. They look like a hardware store's back room, not an industrial warehouse.

## 4. Was the CTA always findable?

**Yes.** On mobile the sticky header kept « Consultation gratuite » plus the phone icon in view at every one of the 17 skim stops (`m03`–`m19`). On desktop the full « Demandez une consultation gratuite » plus the phone number sits in the header the whole time. There are in-page CTAs after the problem section, the process section and the FAQ.

The one weak spot is on mobile. Tapping the hero CTA lands me at the **top of the navy section**, so I re-read the pitch, and the first field (focused) sits at the very bottom edge, 788–836px in an 844px viewport (`m30-after-cta-tap.png`). On a real phone the keyboard then shoves everything around. On desktop the same CTA lands perfectly, with the whole form and submit button in one view (`d40-after-header-cta.png`).

## 5. Form friction

The form is good, and I don't say that often.
- There are 5 required fields plus an optional project box. That's short enough for a free consultation. On desktop it fits in one screen as a two-column grid.
- I got the right keyboards: `tel` for the phone and `email` with no autocapitalise for the email. `autocomplete` is set on name, organisation, email, tel and city, so autofill would do most of it.
- **The mistake:** I typed « 418 555 123 ». The error appeared as soon as I left the field: « Entrez un numéro de téléphone valide (10 chiffres). » It's red, has an icon, says exactly what's wrong and is right under the field (`m31`). Submitting anyway kept focus on the phone field (`m33`, log: `active: lead-phone`). Fixing it cleared the error while I typed (`m34`). « (418) 555-1234 » with brackets and a dash was accepted on desktop. The desktop bad email « m.gagnon@usinagebeauce » got a helpful example message (`d41`).
- The consent line under the button is plain and short. « Vos données restent confidentielles » beside the form helps.
- Small things: the submit label wraps to two lines on mobile (« Demander ma / consultation gratuite »), and nothing reformats my phone number. Neither one bothered me.

## 6. Thank-you page (`m40`, `m41`, `d50`)

It does the job. It confirms the request, repeats the 1-business-day promise, gives three « Et maintenant? » steps and the phone number for urgent cases. I'd close the tab feeling handled.

Two things look off:
- The check-mark circle and the « — Demande reçue » eyebrow sit **side by side**, off-centre above a centred headline. It looks like a layout slip, not a design choice.
- « Retour à la page » is the only button. Which page? Back to what?

## 7. The 3 things I'd change first

1. **Put a real rack photo in the first mobile screen and shrink the cookie sheet** (USER-1, USER-5). The ad is visual, so the page should be too.
2. **Add one piece of hard proof**, either a named project with a number or a customer quote, and caption the gallery with client and city (USER-3). Ask Pedlex for it; it's the difference between « credible » and « call them today ».
3. **Cut the mobile scroll between the stats and the form** with compact feature cards (USER-2). It's 3,200px of cards I didn't read.

## Findings

### USER-1 · major · hero
**What:** At 390×844 the first viewport is all text: eyebrow, a 3-line H1, the sub-headline and two body paragraphs. The hero photo starts at y=804, below the fold. With the cookie banner open, the main CTA (y 557–613) has its bottom edge under the banner (top 602). The 5-second view contains no image of racking at all.
**Where:** 390×844, `qa/shots/user-r1/m01-first-viewport-5s.png`, `m02-after-decline.png`, `m03-skim-y450.png` (the photo only appears after the first scroll)
**Why it matters:** The visitor comes from a video ad. A text-only first screen breaks the visual continuity and is the moment a mobile skimmer bounces.
**Suggested fix:** Below 768px in `src/styles/sections/hero.css`, move the `.hero` media above the copy (CSS `order`) or between the H1 and the lead, at a shorter aspect ratio (e.g. 16/9, still `fetchpriority=high`). Or keep the order and visually collapse the second body paragraph on mobile. Don't rewrite copy; do it with layout only.

### USER-2 · major · problem, why
**What:** On mobile the 4 problem cards and 6 « Pourquoi Pedlex » cards are each a full-width card with a 48px icon tile above the title. That makes the problem section 1,524px and why 1,714px. The page is 10,453px long and the first form field is at 7,657px (about 9 screens). « 50 ans », « clé en main » and « sur mesure » each appear three times (hero trust line, stats, why).
**Where:** 390×844, `qa/shots/user-r1/m05-skim-y1550.png`, `m06-skim-y2200.png`, `m11-skim-y4950.png`, `m12-skim-y5550.png`, `m13-skim-y6200.png`
**Why it matters:** This is where my skim turned into mindless thumb-flicking. Every extra screen before the form costs conversions.
**Suggested fix:** Add a backwards-compatible `compact` variant to the `featureCard` macro in `components/ui.njk`. On mobile it puts the icon inline to the left of the h3 and uses smaller padding tokens; use it for `problem` and `why` below 768px. Optionally show « Pourquoi Pedlex » as a 2-column grid of title-only items on mobile.

### USER-3 · major · trust, gallery, why
**What:** There's no customer evidence anywhere on the page: no testimonial, no named project, no before/after figure. The only result claim is « Jusqu'à 40 % d'espace récupéré dans certains projets ». Gallery captions are product categories only (« Palettiers sélectifs », « Centre de distribution », …) with no client, place or outcome.
**Where:** 390 and 1440, `qa/shots/user-r1/m04-skim-y1050.png`, `m08-skim-y3200.png`, `d06-skim-y2500.png`, `d22-gallery-end.png`, `d09-skim-y4450.png`
**Why it matters:** As a sceptical buyer, logos tell me who bought, but not that it worked. Proof is what moves me from « later » to « now ».
**Suggested fix:** This is a client/agency content request (add it to PROJECT.md §9): one short testimonial or a 1-line case result. Once supplied, add an optional `caption.detail` (client · city · result) to the gallery items in `src/content/racking/fr.json` and render it under the existing caption in `sections/gallery.njk`.

### USER-4 · minor · why
**What:** The badge row is titled « Accréditations en santé et sécurité » and shows the CNESST logo alongside SafeContractor and ComplyWorks. CNESST is Québec's workplace health and safety regulator, not an accrediting body.
**Where:** 390 and 1440, `qa/shots/user-r1/m13-skim-y6200.png`, `d09-skim-y4450.png`
**Why it matters:** To a plant manager who deals with CNESST inspectors, it reads as borrowed authority and dents an otherwise credible section.
**Suggested fix:** Raise it with Pedlex together with §9 question 5. Either drop the CNESST logo, or change the label (microcopy in `src/content/racking/fr.json`) to something accurate such as « Santé et sécurité », with the client's confirmation of what the CNESST mark means for them.

### USER-5 · minor · cookie banner (global)
**What:** On mobile the Law 25 banner is a 226px bottom sheet (about 27% of the viewport). It has 4 lines of body text and full-width stacked padding, and covers the bottom edge of the hero CTA on first load. On desktop it's fine.
**Where:** 390×844, `qa/shots/user-r1/m01-first-viewport-5s.png`; 1440, `d01-first-viewport.png`
**Why it matters:** It's the first thing a mobile visitor has to deal with, and it competes with the one button that matters.
**Suggested fix:** Tighten `src/styles/components/cookie-banner.css` below 768px: smaller text token, reduced padding tokens, title optional. Aim for a sheet of about 150px or less that stays below the hero CTA. Keep both buttons equal weight; that part is good.

### USER-6 · minor · consultation
**What:** On mobile, tapping any CTA scrolls to the top of `#consultation`. The visitor sees the section's eyebrow, H2, two paragraphs and the reassurance list again. The focused « Prénom et nom » field sits at y=788–836 in an 844px viewport, right on the bottom edge. On desktop the same jump shows the whole form (fine).
**Where:** 390×844, `qa/shots/user-r1/m30-after-cta-tap.png`; 1440 for comparison, `d40-after-header-cta.png`
**Why it matters:** I tapped « I want this »; I should land on the fields, not the pitch.
**Suggested fix:** In `src/scripts/components/scroll-to.js`, when the viewport is under 768px, scroll to the form card (`[data-lead-form]`) instead of the section, with a `scroll-margin-top` equal to the sticky header height plus a spacing token. Keep the focus behaviour.

### USER-7 · minor · thank-you page
**What:** The check-mark circle and the « — Demande reçue » eyebrow render on one line, side by side and off-centre, above a centred H1. It looks unintended at both widths.
**Where:** 390, `qa/shots/user-r1/m40-thanks-viewport.png`; 1440, `d50-thanks.png`
**Why it matters:** It's the last impression after converting, and the one visibly unpolished spot I hit.
**Suggested fix:** In `src/styles/pages/thanks.css`, stack the icon above the eyebrow (`flex-direction: column; align-items: center` with a gap token), or centre the eyebrow on its own line under the icon.

### USER-8 · minor · gallery
**What:** Slides 5 and 6 (« Centre de distribution », « Entreposage commercial ») show retail-style shelving with cleaning-product jugs and a bright green pegboard wall. Next to the industrial pallet-racking shots they look like a store's back room.
**Where:** 1440, `qa/shots/user-r1/d22-gallery-end.png`, `d21-gallery-next2.png`
**Why it matters:** They dilute the « industrial, serious integrator » impression for a manufacturing buyer.
**Suggested fix:** Ask Pedlex for two more industrial pallet-racking install photos to replace them in `assets-source/racking/`, then update the gallery list in `fr.json`. Until then, keep them as the last slides (they already are).

### USER-9 · nit · hero
**What:** The hero trust line (« 50 ans d'expertise · Solutions sur mesure · Optimisation… ») has a 1px top border with `padding-top: 0`, so the check icons touch the divider.
**Where:** 390, `qa/shots/user-r1/m03-skim-y450.png`; 1440, `d03-skim-y600.png`
**Why it matters:** It looks cramped right under the main CTA.
**Suggested fix:** Add `padding-top` with a spacing token to the trust-line element in `src/styles/sections/hero.css`.

### USER-10 · nit · problem, process
**What:** At 390 the problem and process CTAs wrap to two lines (« Demandez une consultation / gratuite ») with the arrow pushed to the right edge. The hero CTA at the same width fits on one line with the arrow inline. Same macro, two different looks.
**Where:** 390, `qa/shots/user-r1/m07-skim-y2600.png`, `m10-skim-y4450.png` vs `m02-after-decline.png`
**Why it matters:** It's an inconsistent button rendering on a one-CTA page.
**Suggested fix:** Check the `button`/`ctaRow` macro and section CSS for a differing size or arrow layout. Use the hero's sizing for all full-width mobile CTAs.

### USER-11 · nit · footer (both pages)
**What:** At 390 the RBQ licence number breaks mid-number: « Licence RBQ 8226- / 5026-35 ».
**Where:** 390, `qa/shots/user-r1/m19-skim-y9650.png`, `m41-thanks-full.png`
**Why it matters:** A licence number someone might want to verify shouldn't be split.
**Suggested fix:** Wrap the number in a nowrap span in `partials/footer.njk` (or a non-breaking hyphen via `landings.js` typography).

### USER-12 · nit · trust
**What:** On mobile the logo marquee shows only about 2 logos at a time, greyed out (stm, Globocam). A skimmer passing at normal speed sees 2 of the 6.
**Where:** 390, `qa/shots/user-r1/m04-skim-y1050.png`, `m05-skim-y1550.png`; 1440 shows 5–6 at once, `d03-skim-y600.png`
**Why it matters:** Social proof that half-loads on the device most visitors use.
**Suggested fix:** Use a smaller logo max-width token below 768px in `src/styles/sections/trust.css` so 3–4 logos are visible at once.
