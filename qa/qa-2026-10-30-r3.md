## Why mobile reads as amateur

On desktop the page **composes in two dimensions**: head left / cards right, video left / reasons right, a 12-column photo mosaic, a hero whose right half is clear photography. Each section has a shape, and the photos do the work. On a phone every section **collapses into the same single-column stack**: eyebrow → H2 → grey lead → full-width button → a stack of identical boxes. That is the "corporate brochure stack" PROJECT.md §5 retired. Four things make it worse:

1. **The type hierarchy flattens.** On phones the H2 is 28px *regular* and every h3 is 18px *bold* right under it, so card titles weigh as much as the section title (scroll-390-02). The lead is 18px on 29px leading, only 1.14× the 16px body, so it reads as "slightly too big body text", not as a lead.
2. **Photos shrink to thumbnails and placeholders grow.** The hero photo is covered by copy (≈ 48px of clear photo at 390), réalisations are 170×128 thumbnails under captions half their height, the process photo disappears under a 0.9 scrim, while the « Vidéo Pedlex à venir » dashed tag spans 60% of its frame and « Exemple » dashed boxes sit on every quote. The "unfinished" markers are proportionally 2–3× bigger on phones than on desktop.
3. **Chrome crowds the content.** The sticky header carries a 199×44 navy CTA that overruns the gutter by 7px, and a second identical full-width navy CTA sits 300px below it (problem). With the cookie strip (154px) you lose 218px of an 844px screen to chrome.
4. **Little things that a designer reads as unpolished:** a 2-line full-width hero chip with its marker floating between the lines, a 4-line H1 with « simplement » alone on a line, 3-line gallery captions, « O1 » (letter O) indexes, a stray « · » at the start of a footer line, five different gaps between list items.

## Findings

### QA3-1 · major · hero
**What:** At 390×844 the hero is 852px tall in an 800px space (844 − 44 top bar), so the third proof row (« 10 000+ ») is below the fold (first-screen-390). The copy block starts at y=156, so between the logo (bottom ≈132) and the chip there are ≈ 24–48px of clear photo: the photo is only a texture behind text. At 360 the hero is 894px and the CTA sits on the fold (first-screen-360). Within it:
- **Tag chip:** « MAXIMISEZ VOTRE CAPACITÉ D'ENTREPOSAGE » wraps to two lines, so the inline-flex chip stretches to the full 357px width and 51px height. It reads as a dark banner, not a chip, and the 6px marker sits centred between the two lines. On desktop it's a neat one-line 435px chip.
- **H1:** 36.5px, balanced into 4 lines: « Votre entrepôt / est plein… ou / simplement / mal optimisé ? » (line widths 261/234/215/254 of 357). « simplement » sits alone, and the right 100px of the column stays empty, so the title looks narrow and timid next to the full-width lead and button. At 430 it fits 3 lines and looks right (first-screen-430).
- **Lead:** 18.2px / 29px leading, 4 lines; the last line is « déplacements. » alone.
**Where:** 390, 360 · `first-screen-390.png`, `first-screen-360.png`, `section-hero-390.png`; compare `first-screen-430.png`, `first-screen-1440.png`
**Why it matters:** this is the screen the ad click lands on. Overfull and ragged reads as "template that didn't fit".
**Suggested fix:**
- `hero.css`, inside `@media (max-width: 40rem)`: `.hero__tag { display: none; }` (the top bar already says « 50 ans · Entreprise familiale », and the H1 asks the question). Alternative if Josh wants it kept: a short label via a new `c.hero.tagShort` (copy trim, e.g. « Capacité d'entreposage »), with `.hero__tag { width: fit-content; }`. Saves ≈ 75px.
- `00-tokens.css`, phone block (`max-width: 47.99rem`): `--fs-h1: clamp(2rem, 8.7vw, 2.5rem);` gives 31px at 360, 34px at 390 and 37px at 430. « plein… ou simplement » (≈ 10.2em) then fits, for 3 balanced lines at all three widths. Saves ≈ 40px. H1 and H2 never share a screen, so the smaller H1/H2 ratio doesn't hurt.
- Leads: see QA3-2 (`--lh-lead`). Together with QA3-5 the hero drops to ≈ 690px, so the full proof strip shows on the first screen at 390 and the copy actually sits low on the photo.

### QA3-2 · major · global (type scale on phones)
**What:** The computed phone inventory (metrics-390.json) is 13 / 16 / 18.2 / 28 / 36.5. Two problems:
1. **Hierarchy inversion.** H2 = 28px **regular** with −0.03em tracking (`h1, h2 { font-weight: 400 }`); every h3 (`.card__title`, `.process__title`) = 18.2px **bold**. At 390 the bold 18px titles have about the same visual weight as the light 28px H2 above them (scroll-390-02: « Un meilleur accès à l'inventaire » vs « Des entrepôts transformés… »; scroll-390-04: process titles vs « Tout commence par… »). On desktop it works because the gap is 42 regular vs 22 bold.
2. **Lead too close to body.** Lead 18.2 vs body 16 = 1.14×, both regular, lead in `--text-2` grey at `--lh-body` 1.6 (29px leading). Section leads run 2–3 lines of loose grey (« Des systèmes de palettiers conçus / pour augmenter votre capacité et / faciliter… » = 87px). It looks like a second body size, not a deliberate lead.

Also, bold 16 (FAQ questions, trust caption, gallery captions, form labels, reviewer names, why caption) and bold 18 (h3) are two near-identical bold sizes on the same screens.
**Where:** 390, 360 · `scroll-390-01.png`, `scroll-390-02.png`, `scroll-390-04.png`, `metrics-390.json`
**Why it matters:** a clear size contrast is what separates designed from default. Right now the phone scale has four sizes packed between 16 and 28.
**Suggested fix (stays within the four sizes):**
- Phones: h3 uses `--fs-body` (bold). In `02-base.css`, `@media (max-width: 47.99rem) { h3 { font-size: var(--fs-body); } }`; `.card__title` and `.process__title` set `font-size: var(--fs-lead)` explicitly, so change them to inherit at that breakpoint (`card.css`, `process.css`). All bold labels then share one size (16 bold), and 18 is used for leads only.
- `00-tokens.css` phone block: `--fs-display: 1.875rem;` (30px). Below 48rem the clamp already returns 28.0–28.2, so a fixed 30 is cleaner. Re-check wraps: « Pensé pour vos opérations, pas seulement pour vos palettes. » becomes 4 lines at 360 (already 4 there), 3 at 390.
- New token `--lh-lead: 1.45` used by `.section-lead` (03-layout.css) and `.hero__lead` (hero.css) at all widths: 29px → 26px leading. It also tightens desktop slightly, which is fine at 22px.

### QA3-3 · major · header (sticky, phones)
**What:** Once scrolled, the header row at 390 is: logo 96×24 at x=17, call square 44×44 at x=129, CTA 199×44 at x=181 → **right edge 380, but the gutter is at 373** (7px overrun). Content needs 96 + 16 + 44 + 8 + 199 = 363px; the container has 356. The CTA is 51% of the header width and the heaviest object on every phone screen (navy slab, 16px bold). The logo, the brand, is the smallest element. At 360 the call square is hidden (< 24rem) and the CTA sits correctly on the gutter (344), which shows the 390 overrun is a threshold bug.
**Where:** 390 (also any width 384–416px) · `sticky-header-390.png`, `scroll-390-01.png` … `-11.png`
**Why it matters:** an element hanging past the grid edge is the first thing a designer's eye catches, and it shows on every screen after the hero.
**Suggested fix:** `header.css`: move the `.site-header__phone { display: none; }` rule from `max-width: 23.99rem` to `max-width: 26rem` (the call stays in the hero, consultation, footer, and in the top bar on scroll-up). `00-tokens.css`: `--logo-w: clamp(7rem, 1rem + 10vw, 11.5rem)` (112px on phones). If Josh wants the call button kept: `.site-header__inner { gap: var(--space-xs); }` on phones plus CTA `padding-inline: var(--space-xs)` brings it to exactly 356, but that's cramped. Prefer dropping it.

### QA3-4 · major · problem
**What:** On phones the section is eyebrow → H2 → 3-line lead → full-width navy CTA (357×56) → **four bordered white boxes** (357×180 each, 24px padding, 40px tinted icon tile beside an 18px bold title, body under). Together that's 1 121px, 13% of the page. This is the "white icon-chip feature-card grid" PROJECT.md §5 lists under *Retire*. On desktop it survives because the 2×2 sits beside a sticky head; on phones it's a plain stack of identical boxes inside a 17px gutter (box-in-band-in-screen). The CTA comes *before* the content it should conclude, and lands ≈ 300px under the identical header CTA « Consultation gratuite » (scroll-390-01 shows both on one screen).
**Where:** 390, 360 · `scroll-390-01.png`, `scroll-390-02.png`, `section-problem-390.png`; desktop `section-problem-1440.png`
**Why it matters:** this is the first content section after the hero, and the most template-looking block on the page.
**Suggested fix:** `problem.css`, `@media (max-width: 39.99rem)`: `.problem__cards { gap: 0; border-top: var(--border); }` `.problem__cards .card { padding: var(--space-m) 0; border: 0; border-bottom: var(--border); background: none; border-radius: 0; }` `.problem__cards .card:hover { transform: none; box-shadow: none; }` `.problem__cards .card__icon { background: none; width: auto; height: auto; color: var(--color-accent); }`. That gives the same hairline language as the FAQ and the proof strip, and saves ≈ 100px. CTA: on phones render it after the cards (`.problem__intro { display: contents; }` + `order` on the cta-row inside `.problem__layout`), or hide `.problem__intro .cta-row` under 40rem since the sticky header CTA is on screen.

### QA3-5 · major · hero (proof strip)
**What:** Phones use a 2-column subgrid: figure (28px bold) | label (18.2px regular, `--fs-lead`). The figure column takes the width of « 30 à 50 % » (≈ 150px), so labels get ≈ 167px and wrap: « d'espace récupéré / dans certains projets », « d'expertise en / entreposage »; at 360 « d'espace / récupéré dans / certains projets » (3 lines) and « entreprises / servies ». Labels baseline-align to the first line of the figure, so 2–3-line labels hang below the figures and every row is a different height (66/66/48). Strip = 198px at 390, 242 at 360. The 18px labels are as big as the hero lead above them and compete with the figures.
**Where:** 390, 360, 430 · `first-screen-390.png`, `scroll-360-01.png`, `first-screen-430.png`
**Why it matters:** the proof is the hook for a sceptical skimmer. Ragged 3-line labels make it look like a table, not a stat band.
**Suggested fix:** `hero.css`, phones (≤ 47.99rem): `.hero__proof-label { font-size: var(--fs-body); }`, `.hero__proof-item { align-items: center; padding-block: var(--space-2xs); }`, `column-gap: var(--space-m)`. Labels become 1 line at 390/430 and ≤ 2 at 360, and the strip shrinks to ≈ 150px. (PROJECT §5 says labels at lead size; on phones body size is the right call, so desktop keeps lead.)

### QA3-14 · minor · global (spacing rhythm)

## Desktop vs phone: what desktop does well that phones lose

| Desktop (1440) | Phone (390) |
|---|---|
| Hero: copy on the left, right half clear photography; 3-column proof strip with hairline dividers | Copy covers the whole photo (≈ 48px clear); proof becomes a 3-row table |
| Problem: head + CTA left (sticky), 2×2 cards right: a composition | Head → CTA → 4 stacked boxes: a list |
| Réalisations: 12-col mosaic, 1 large + 4, captions short on one line | 170px thumbnails, 3-line captions |
| Why: tall video frame left, reasons 2×2 right; the placeholder tag is a small note | Video 223px tall, the tag spans 60% of it |
| Type: 42 regular H2 vs 22 bold h3 vs 17 body = clear steps | 28 regular vs 18 bold vs 16 = steps collapse |
| Sections at 90px padding with calm bands | 42px padding, zebra bands, varied gaps |

The phone fixes above (de-box, de-tile, tighten the hero, re-step the type, shrink placeholder markers) bring the phone back to the desktop's *restraint* without changing the content order.
