## Verdict: **approve with changes**

The page looks like a serious industrial company. It is navy-led, restrained and precise, with one clear action and no navigation. It is clearly not a copy of Federal Steel: theirs is a dark page with an amber accent, a photo collage and two different CTAs, while ours is white and navy, editorial, and has a single CTA. We would put our name on this structure.

We would not launch it as-is, for two reasons:
1. **It undersells Pedlex.** We have 10 000+ companies served, 98 % satisfaction, 50 years as a *family* company and big-name clients. Nearly none of that shows. The stats band leads with "Clé en main", the logos are mid-tier, and the gallery never names a single project. Federal Steel shows "5,000+ Satisfied Clients" and Air Canada and Pepsi logos above the fold. We beat them on paper, but they look bigger on screen.
2. **Four statements need our confirmation before they go public:** "1 jour ouvrable", "jusqu'à 40 %", the "Accréditations" label next to the CNESST logo, and a gallery caption calling a retail store a "Centre de distribution".

## What represents Pedlex well
- **Palette and restraint.** Navy `#0F3A5F` with greys, crisp buttons and condensed caps labels. It reads as the same family as pedlex.com without the corporate clutter (webinar bar, chat bubble, shop button).
- **The hero headline matches the ad** (« Votre entrepôt est plein… ou simplement mal optimisé ? »). The CTA and phone number are visible at once on desktop (`scroll-1440-01.png`).
- **The process section photo** (installer in a harness on a scissor lift, `scroll-1440-05.png`) is the most "Pedlex" image on the page: real people, safety, precision. More of this.
- **The form section is reassuring and short.** It has the "Sans frais ni engagement" line, the confidentiality line and a phone fallback (`scroll-1440-08.png`).
- **The footer facts are correct:** address, 514 and 1 888 numbers, info@, RBQ 8226-5026-35, and « Votre partenaire en optimisation d'espace depuis 1976 » (an official tagline).
- **One CTA, no navigation.** "Demandez une consultation gratuite" is everywhere, and the sticky header CTA is always reachable.
- **Real photos only.** No stock, no renders.

## What doesn't (yet)
- **Proof is weak.** The stats band, logos and gallery captions are covered in CLIENT-1, 5, 6 and 11.
- **No family story and no 50th-anniversary moment.** "50 ans" appears three times, but only as a generic number (CLIENT-9).
- **Missing visual signature on mobile.** The first phone screen has no photo and the logo is soft (CLIENT-7, 8).
- **Typography is softer than our brand.** Barlow in mixed case is gentler than pedlex.com's heavy, wide caps headlines (CLIENT-10).

## Québec French and tone
- **The French is natural Québec French:** « courriel », « témoins », « palettiers », « échéancier », « sans frais », « Licence RBQ ». FR typography is correct: narrow no-break spaces before ? and %, curly apostrophes, a true ellipsis character. "Cantilever" is the usual industry term here and is fine.
- **The tone is professional and reassuring, as intended.** It is a bit repetitive: "consultation gratuite" appears about 12 times, and "optimiser/optimisation" appears in almost every block. That is Maïeutyk's copy, so we only flag it and don't ask for changes.
- **Luxy microcopy:** « Retour à la page » on the thank-you page is vague. « Retour à la page Palettiers » or « Revenir au site » would be clearer (see CLIENT-16).

## Change requests

### CLIENT-1 · major · stats
**What:** The navy proof band shows « Jusqu'à 40 % », « 50 ans » and « Clé en main ». "Clé en main" is a phrase, not proof. Our current reference figures (2026 catalogue, confirmed by Kaisey as the reference) are **10 000+ entreprises servies, 98 % de satisfaction, 5 000+ plans, 30+ spécialistes**, and none of them appear. Federal Steel puts "5,000+ Satisfied Clients" and "75 years" under its hero; we have double their client count and don't say it.
**Where:** 1440 `qa/shots/client-r1/scroll-1440-02.png`, `section-stats-1440.png`; 390 `scroll-390-02.png`; competitor `ref-federalsteel-1440-top.png`
**Why it matters:** This is the proof the skimming plant manager sees right after the hero. As it stands, it is our weakest band against the competitor.
**Suggested fix:** Ask Maïeutyk/Camille to approve replacing « Clé en main » with « 10 000+ entreprises servies » (and optionally swapping 40 % for 98 % satisfaction; see CLIENT-2). Then update `c.stats` in `src/content/racking/fr.json` and log the change in PROJECT.md §9. The counters already support numbers.

### CLIENT-2 · major · stats / faq
**What:** « Jusqu'à 40 % d'espace récupéré dans certains projets » appears in the stats band, and the FAQ repeats it: « Chez certains clients, cela a permis de récupérer jusqu'à 40 % d'espace supplémentaire ». No project backs it on the page, and the Général copy uses « 30 à 50 % ».
**Where:** 1440 `scroll-1440-02.png`; FAQ `qa/shots/client-r1/faq-1440-all-open.png`
**Why it matters:** This is a public performance claim in a paid campaign, and a competitor or client could ask us to back it up.
**Suggested fix:** Pedlex (Francis or sales) names the project or projects behind 40 % and aligns the Racking and Général figures (PROJECT.md §9 Q3). If we can't, replace the figure with a catalogue figure (CLIENT-1). No code change until then.

### CLIENT-3 · major · consultation, merci
**What:** « Réponse d'un expert en 1 jour ouvrable » (form reassurance) and « communiquera avec vous dans un délai d'un jour ouvrable » (thank-you page) commit us to a 1-business-day SLA on every lead.
**Where:** 1440 `scroll-1440-08.png`; `merci-1440.png`; 390 `scroll-390-11.png`, `merci-390.png`
**Why it matters:** Campaign weeks can bring volume spikes. A broken promise on the first contact is worse than no promise.
**Suggested fix:** Pedlex confirms who answers (Alex's inbox and sales) and whether 1 day holds (PROJECT.md §9 Q2). If not, soften the line in `src/content/_shared/fr.json` (it is Luxy microcopy), e.g. « Un expert vous rappelle rapidement » or « sous 24 à 48 h ouvrables ».

### CLIENT-4 · major · why
**What:** The row label reads « ACCRÉDITATIONS EN SANTÉ ET SÉCURITÉ » followed by the CNESST, SafeContractor and ComplyWorks logos. CNESST is the government health-and-safety board, not an accrediting body, and using its logo under "Accréditations" implies an endorsement. SafeContractor and ComplyWorks are contractor prequalification programs, so "accréditation" is a stretch there too.
**Where:** 1440 `scroll-1440-07.png`, `section-why-1440.png`; 390 `scroll-390-10.png`
**Why it matters:** Using a regulator's logo with a claim we can't document is a legal and reputational risk.
**Suggested fix:** Pedlex confirms what each logo represents (e.g. CNESST attestation de conformité, SafeContractor/ComplyWorks prequalification). Then relabel in `src/content/racking/fr.json` (e.g. « Conformité et préqualification SST »), or drop the CNESST logo if no attestation exists (PROJECT.md §9 Q5).

### CLIENT-5 · major · gallery
**What:** The « Réalisations » carousel captions are product types only (« Palettiers sélectifs », « Cantilever pour produits longs », « Entreposage à haute densité »…). There is no client, city or number. We have approved case studies with numbers, for example Groupe Ricochet (4 000+ palettes, palettiers de 32 pi en 3 semaines) and Airbus (palettiers + cantilever, 26 pi).
**Where:** 1440 `scroll-1440-04.png`, `gallery-1440-end.png`; 390 `scroll-390-06.png`, `gallery-390-end.png`
**Why it matters:** "Real installs" only convinces if the visitor can tell they are real. Unnamed photos read like the generic gallery Federal Steel uses.
**Suggested fix:** Pedlex identifies the site behind each of racking-1, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 9. For named ones, the caption becomes « Client · ville · chiffre » (e.g. « Groupe Ricochet · 4 000+ palettes »), set in the gallery items in `src/content/racking/fr.json`. Only name a client where the photo truly is that project.

### CLIENT-6 · major · gallery
**What:** Slides 5 and 6 (« Centre de distribution », « Entreposage commercial ») are two angles of the same small retail store: green pegboard walls, cleaning products, mop buckets. The unused racking-7 is a third shot of that same store. Slide 5's caption calls it a distribution centre, which it visibly isn't. These two slides look like a hardware store, not industrial pallet racking.
**Where:** 1440 `qa/shots/client-r1/gallery-1440-end.png`; 390 `gallery-390-end.png`
**Why it matters:** The caption is inaccurate, and the photos are off-brand (green walls, retail clutter) in a campaign aimed at plant and warehouse managers.
**Suggested fix:** Keep at most one of the store shots, captioned truthfully (e.g. « Commerce de produits d'entretien »). Ask Pedlex for 1–2 large industrial installs (Ricochet, Airbus) to replace the other. Content-only change in `src/content/racking/fr.json` plus `assets-source/racking/`.

### CLIENT-7 · major · hero (mobile)
**What:** At 390×844 the first screen is all text: eyebrow, H1, subhead, two paragraphs, CTA, phone and trust line. The hero installation photo starts about 800px down, below the fold. On a first visit, the cookie banner also covers the bottom third, including the CTA.
**Where:** 390 `qa/shots/client-r1/scroll-390-01.png`, `first-390-banner.png`, `scroll-390-02.png`
**Why it matters:** Most clicks come from a video ad on a phone. Landing on a wall of text, with no Pedlex installation in sight, breaks the continuity with the video.
**Suggested fix:** In `src/styles/sections/hero.css` below 768px, place the hero media between the H1 and the subhead (or directly under the header) at a shorter aspect ratio (about 16:9, roughly 200px tall). Keep the drift. The copy stays untouched; only the order changes.

### CLIENT-8 · major · header / footer

**Where:** 1440@2× crop `qa/shots/client-r1/header-logo-1440@2x-crop.png`; 1440 `scroll-1440-01.png`
**Why it matters:** A fuzzy logo on a "precise" brand page undercuts the whole message.
**Suggested fix:** Replace it with the vector « Logo Pedlex sans slogan » (navy + WHITE) from Drive, exported to SVG in `assets-source/brand/`. Treat this as a go-live condition (already listed in PROJECT.md §9).

### CLIENT-10 · minor · global typography
**What:** The headlines are Barlow semi-bold in mixed case. pedlex.com uses heavy, wide, all-caps headlines (« EXPERTS EN SOLUTIONS DE L'ENTREPOSAGE… DEPUIS 1976 »). Next to our site, the landing page feels gentler and more "SaaS" than Pedlex.
**Where:** 1440 `scroll-1440-01.png` vs `qa/shots/client-r1/ref-pedlex-com-1440-mid.png`
**Why it matters:** Brand continuity from pedlex.com and the 2026 catalogue.
**Suggested fix:** Swap in Scania Sans before go-live (already on the to-do list). Meanwhile, consider the condensed caps (`--font-display`-style token) for the stats numbers and one display moment, without uppercasing the H1 copy.

### CLIENT-11 · major · trust
**What:** Six logos are shown: STM, Globocam, Nordic, Omnifab, Distribution Pro-Expert and Airbus. Half are small regional names, and on mobile only about two are visible at a time, with STM faded at the edge. Logos already approved for ads include Bombardier, Air Canada, CAE, Hydro-Québec, PepsiCo, Purolator, Cascades and Costco. Federal Steel shows Air Canada and Pepsi right under its hero.
**Where:** 1440 `scroll-1440-02.png`, `section-trust-1440.png`; 390 `scroll-390-03.png`; competitor `ref-federalsteel-1440-top.png`
**Why it matters:** Recognisable names are the fastest trust signal for a sceptical manager.
**Suggested fix:** Pedlex/Luxy supply 4+ hi-res or vector logos from the approved list into `assets-source/clients/` and add them to the trust items in `src/content/racking/fr.json`. Lead with the strongest names (Airbus, Bombardier, Air Canada, Hydro-Québec, STM).
